SDXL reads prompts differently from SD 1.5. It understands short descriptive sentences, needs fewer "masterpiece, best quality" tags, and is far more sensitive to resolution. This guide gives you a formula, ready prompts and the settings that matter.

## The SDXL prompt formula

```text
[subject], [action or pose], [setting], [lighting], [camera or medium], [style details]
```

Put the most important thing first and describe it concretely. "An old fisherman mending a net on a wooden pier" works better than "fisherman, old, net, pier, detailed, 8k".

A full example:

```text
an old fisherman mending a red net on a wooden pier, early morning fog, soft diffused light, 85mm lens, shallow depth of field, muted film colors
```

## Copy-paste SDXL prompts

Each prompt is paired with a short negative prompt. For longer lists, see the [negative prompt guide](/blog/2025-11-29-stable-diffusion-negative-prompts-guide).

### Portrait photo

```text
close-up portrait of a woman in her 40s with freckles and grey-streaked hair, window light from the left, neutral background, 85mm lens, natural skin texture, documentary photography
```

```text
cartoon, illustration, 3d render, plastic skin, airbrushed, oversaturated, watermark, text
```

### Product shot

```text
a matte black ceramic coffee mug on a light oak table, soft morning light, clean beige background, product photography, centered composition, subtle shadow
```

```text
cluttered background, dust, fingerprints, harsh reflections, text, watermark, lowres
```

### Landscape

```text
alpine lake at sunrise, mist over the water, pine forest on the shore, snow-capped peaks reflected in the lake, wide-angle landscape photography, crisp detail
```

```text
people, buildings, power lines, oversaturated, blurry, text, watermark
```

### Interior

```text
a small Scandinavian living room, white walls, light wood floor, linen sofa, large window with afternoon sun, potted fig tree, architectural photography
```

```text
distorted perspective, warped lines, clutter, people, text, watermark
```

### Illustration

```text
a fox reading a book under a mushroom in the rain, children's book illustration, watercolor and ink, soft pastel palette, white paper texture
```

```text
photograph, photorealistic, 3d render, dark, gritty, text, watermark
```

### Anime style

```text
a girl with short silver hair standing on a rooftop at dusk, city lights below, wind in her coat, anime style, cel shading, vivid sunset colors
```

```text
photorealistic, 3d, lowres, bad anatomy, extra fingers, text, watermark
```

### Fantasy scene

```text
an ancient stone bridge over a misty gorge, a lone traveler with a lantern, towering cliffs, epic fantasy concept art, volumetric light, muted greens and greys
```

```text
modern buildings, cars, cartoon, oversaturated, text, watermark
```

Base SDXL produces these styles on its own. Fine-tuned checkpoints (photo, anime, illustration) usually push one style much further; check each model page for its preferred prompt format.

## Settings that matter more than words

### Resolution

SDXL works best at about one megapixel. The Diffusers documentation says most SDXL checkpoints work best at 1024×1024; 768 and 512 work but look worse, and anything below 512 is not recommended.

| Aspect (approx.) | Size |
| --- | --- |
| 1:1 square | 1024 × 1024 |
| 4:5 / 5:4 | 896 × 1152 / 1152 × 896 |
| 2:3 / 3:2 | 832 × 1216 / 1216 × 832 |
| 9:16 / 16:9 | 768 × 1344 / 1344 × 768 |

These sizes keep the pixel count close to 1024 × 1024. Generating at 512 × 512, a habit from SD 1.5, is the most common reason SDXL images look soft or broken.

### Guidance (CFG) and steps

Diffusers uses a default guidance scale of 5.0 for SDXL. A range of about 4–8 is a reasonable starting point; very high values tend to burn colors and contrast. The Diffusers docs also note that DPM++ samplers below 50 steps can produce visual artifacts with SDXL and suggest enabling Karras sigmas, which most UIs list as "DPM++ 2M Karras".

### Long prompts

The CLIP text encoders read 75 tokens at a time. AUTOMATIC1111 and similar UIs split longer prompts into chunks automatically, but words near the start of the prompt still tend to matter most. Keep the subject first.

## What is different from SD 1.5

- **Two text encoders.** SDXL uses CLIP ViT-L and OpenCLIP ViT-bigG. In Diffusers you can send a second prompt to the second encoder with `prompt_2` (for example, the subject in `prompt` and the style in `prompt_2`). If you leave it empty, both encoders get the same prompt, which is what most UIs do.
- **Fewer quality tags.** Lists like "masterpiece, best quality, ultra detailed, 8k" were a workaround for SD 1.5. In SDXL they add little; spend those words on the subject, light and medium.
- **Sentences work.** Short natural phrases ("a dog sleeping on a red sofa") are understood. Tag lists still work, especially on anime checkpoints trained on tags.

## The refiner: optional

SDXL 1.0 shipped with a separate refiner model that adds fine detail in the last, low-noise part of generation. In the Diffusers "ensemble of experts" setup, the base model runs the first 80% of denoising (`denoising_end=0.8`) and the refiner runs the rest (`denoising_start=0.8`).

The refiner needs extra memory and time. Many fine-tuned SDXL checkpoints are published to be used without it, so check the model page before adding it.

## Making a word stronger

In AUTOMATIC1111 and ComfyUI, `(word:1.2)` increases a word's weight and `(word:0.8)` reduces it:

```text
a cottage in a meadow, (wildflowers:1.3), (fog:0.7), golden hour
```

Stay between about 0.7 and 1.4. Larger values often distort the image. This syntax does not work in Midjourney or in plain API calls.

## What SDXL still struggles with

The official SDXL model card lists these limitations:

- It cannot render legible text. Add text afterwards in an editor.
- Faces and people may not be generated properly, especially small faces in wide shots.
- Complex compositions ("a red cube on top of a blue sphere left of a green cone") are hard.
- It does not reach perfect photorealism.

For layout problems, [regional prompting](/blog/2026-01-04-stable-diffusion-regional-prompting-guide) gives each part of the image its own prompt. For variety across many images, [wildcards](/blog/2026-01-25-stable-diffusion-wildcards-guide) swap words automatically. Useful add-ons are covered in the [extensions guide](/blog/2026-02-13-best-stable-diffusion-extensions).
