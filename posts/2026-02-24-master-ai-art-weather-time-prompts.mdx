Time of day sets the light. Weather sets the atmosphere. Together they decide the mood of an image more than the subject does. Every block below has a copy button; swap in your own subject.

## Quick reference

| Time | Light words that match |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-dawn / dusk blue hour | deep blue sky, cool ambient light, city lights turning on, no direct sun |
| Sunrise | low warm sun, long soft shadows, pink and orange sky, morning haze |
| Midday | high sun, hard short shadows, bright neutral light, saturated colors |
| Golden hour | low golden sunlight, long shadows, warm rim light, glowing edges |
| Sunset | sun at the horizon, orange and purple sky, silhouettes |
| Night | moonlight, street lamps, neon, deep shadows, starry sky |

Golden hour and blue hour have no official definition. Photographers generally mean the period just after sunrise and before sunset (golden) and the short twilight when the sun is just below the horizon (blue). Civil twilight, the official term closest to blue hour, ends when the sun's center is 6 degrees below the horizon.

## Tips

- **Keep light cues consistent.** "Midday" with "long shadows" or "golden hour" with "blue overcast light" contradict each other. Pick one time and one light quality.
- **Weather changes the surfaces too.** Rain means wet, reflective ground; snow means muted color and soft edges; fog means detail fading with distance. Adding these details makes the weather believable.
- **Indoors, show the weather through a window.** "Rain streaking down the window, grey light inside the room" works better than "rain" alone, which can put rain inside.
- **Remove what you do not want.** If the model keeps adding rain or lens flare, put `rain, lens flare` in the negative field. See the [negative prompt lists](/blog/2025-11-29-stable-diffusion-negative-prompts-guide).
- **Combine with a lens.** Wide lenses show the sky and the weather; telephoto lenses stack fog and rain into layers. See the [lens prompt cheat sheet](/blog/2026-02-16-master-ai-art-lenses-prompts-guide).
