Writing "a sad woman" usually gives a generic, slightly sad face. Emotion becomes convincing when the prompt describes what you would actually see: the face, the posture and the scene around the person. This guide gives copy-paste prompts for each layer.

## The three layers of emotion

| Layer | What to describe | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Face | Eyes, brows, mouth, tears, tension | furrowed brow, clenched jaw |
| Body | Posture, hands, movement, distance | shoulders hunched, arms wrapped around knees |
| Scene | Light, color, weather, space | cold blue light, empty room, rain on the window |

You do not need all three every time. A close-up portrait relies on the face; a wide shot relies on body and scene.

## Prompts for core emotions

Psychologist Paul Ekman's research describes seven emotions with universal facial expressions: enjoyment, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, disgust and contempt. They make a good starting set.

### Joy

```text
genuine wide smile reaching the eyes, crinkled eyes, head tilted back in laughter, relaxed shoulders
```

```text
a grandmother laughing with her grandchildren at a garden table, warm afternoon sun, bright colors, candid moment
```

### Sadness

```text
downcast eyes, inner corners of the eyebrows raised, trembling lips, tears on the cheeks, shoulders slumped
```

```text
a man sitting alone on a train station bench at dusk, head lowered, rain on the platform, cold blue light, empty space around him
```

### Anger

```text
furrowed brow, glaring eyes, flared nostrils, clenched jaw, tense neck, fists clenched
```

```text
a chef shouting in a steaming kitchen, red-faced, pointing, harsh overhead light, chaotic background
```

### Fear

```text
wide eyes with visible whites, raised eyebrows, mouth slightly open, body pulled back, hands raised defensively
```

```text
a girl hiding behind a door, peering into a dark hallway, single flickering light, deep shadows, cold green tint
```

### Surprise

```text
raised eyebrows, wide open eyes, dropped jaw, hand over mouth, frozen mid-movement
```

```text
a woman opening a gift box, sparkles of light on her face, mouth open in surprise, warm living room lights
```

### Disgust

```text
wrinkled nose, raised upper lip, squinting eyes, head turned away, hand pushing something away
```

### Contempt

```text
one corner of the mouth raised in a smirk, half-lidded eyes, chin raised, looking down at the viewer
```

## Prompts for moods

These work with or without people in the scene.

### Calm

```text
a still lake at dawn, soft pastel sky, mist over the water, a single wooden boat, quiet minimal composition
```

### Melancholy

```text
an empty seaside cafe in late autumn, rain on the windows, a cold cup of coffee, muted grey-blue tones
```

### Nostalgia

```text
a child's bicycle leaning against a sunlit garden fence, faded film colors, light leaks, warm late-summer afternoon
```

### Loneliness

```text
a single figure on a vast snowy plain, tiny in the frame, overcast sky, no other signs of life
```

### Hope

```text
sunlight breaking through storm clouds onto a ruined village, a green sprout growing from the rubble, warm light on cold tones
```

### Tension

```text
two people facing each other across a dark interrogation table, single overhead lamp, hard shadows, tight framing
```

### Awe

```text
a tiny climber looking up at an enormous glacier wall, cathedral-like ice, shafts of light, epic scale
```

### Mystery

```text
a narrow foggy alley at night, one lit doorway at the end, a silhouette standing still, wet cobblestones
```

### Coziness

```text
a reading nook by a rainy window, knitted blanket, steaming tea, warm lamp light, soft textures
```

### Excitement

```text
a crowd at a summer music festival, hands in the air, confetti, colorful stage lights, motion blur
```

## Tips

- **Describe signs, not labels.** "Tears on the cheeks, lowered gaze" beats "very sad".
- **Control intensity with words.** Use `subtle smile`, `faint frown` or `slight unease` for quiet emotion; `laughing out loud`, `screaming` or `sobbing` for strong emotion.
- **Get close enough to see the face.** Expressions are lost in wide shots. Use "close-up portrait" or a portrait lens term; see the [lens prompt cheat sheet](/blog/2026-02-16-master-ai-art-lenses-prompts-guide).
- **Let light carry the mood.** Warm, soft light reads as safe and happy; cold, hard or low light reads as sad or tense. The [weather and time-of-day prompts](/blog/2026-02-24-master-ai-art-weather-time-prompts) pair well with these.
- **Stop the default smile.** For serious portraits, add `smiling, smile` to the negative prompt. More lists are in the [negative prompt guide](/blog/2025-11-29-stable-diffusion-negative-prompts-guide).
- **Do not mix opposite cues.** "Joyful" with "dark gloomy lighting" gives an unclear result unless the contrast is the point.
