Ukiyo-e is usually translated as "pictures of the floating world." According to the Library of Congress, the art developed in Edo (now Tokyo) during the Edo period (1615–1868), and each print was the work of four people: a publisher, an artist who drew the design in ink, a carver who cut it into woodblocks, and a printer who applied the colors. Those woodblocks are the key to the look, and the key to a good prompt.

## What makes a print look like ukiyo-e

| Trait | Prompt words |
| --- | --- |
| Carved outlines | bold black outlines, woodblock line work, crisp contours |
| Flat color areas | flat areas of color, limited palette, no gradients except sky bands |
| Paper and printing | visible paper texture, wood grain texture, slight color misregistration |
| Traditional colors | indigo blue, vermilion red, muted ochre, soft green, off-white paper |
| Composition | strong diagonals, cropped foreground objects, high horizon, layered depth |
| Sky | bokashi gradient sky, a band of deep blue fading to white |

The Library of Congress also notes that early prints were monochrome, printed in black ink, while later works were built up in layers of printed color, some with embossing. Suzuki Harunobu is credited with developing full-color *nishiki-e*, or "brocade prints." Use `nishiki-e color woodblock print` for the colorful look, or `sumizuri-e black ink woodblock print` for the early monochrome style.

## Base style prompt

```text
traditional Japanese ukiyo-e woodblock print, bold black outlines, flat areas of color, indigo and vermilion palette, visible paper texture, Edo period style
```

Put your subject in front of this and it will carry most of the style.

## Famous-places composition

Hiroshige and Hokusai both made series of famous places. The Library of Congress points out how Hiroshige crops a boat and its passenger in the extreme foreground with the view far behind. That trick is easy to prompt:

```text
ukiyo-e print, a boat and a boatman cropped at the edge of the extreme foreground, a temple and a bridge small in the far distance, high horizon, flat color
```

```text
ukiyo-e print in the style of Utagawa Hiroshige, a station on an old highway at dusk, travelers on the road, bands of color in the sky
```

```text
ukiyo-e print in the style of Katsushika Hokusai, Mount Fuji seen through the frame of a large wooden barrel being built, craftsman at work
```

## Figures: beauties and actors

The major genres were beauties (*bijin-ga*), kabuki actors and, later, landscapes.

```text
ukiyo-e bijin-ga print of a woman in a patterned kimono reading a letter, elegant pose, plain background, delicate line work, soft colors
```

```text
ukiyo-e print of a kabuki actor in a dramatic pose, exaggerated facial expression, bold costume pattern, mica-like background, half-length portrait
```

```text
ukiyo-e print of a samurai warrior in armor drawing his sword, swirling clouds, dynamic composition, bold outlines
```

## Negative prompt

```text
3d render, photo, realistic shading, gradient shading on faces, anime, manga, chinese ink painting, oil painting, blurry
```

Readable Japanese text is rarely generated correctly, so add `text, letters, signature` if you want a clean print without fake inscriptions. More lists are in the [negative prompt guide](/blog/2025-11-29-stable-diffusion-negative-prompts-guide).

## Tips

- **Say "woodblock print," not just "Japanese art."** The printing method drives the look: outlines, flat color, paper texture.
- **Keep the palette short.** Three or four named colors look more authentic than a full rainbow.
- **Crop boldly.** A big foreground object cut by the frame with a small distant view is a strong ukiyo-e composition.
- **Separate it from anime.** Put `anime, manga` in the negative field if faces start to look modern.
- **Combine with ink.** For a black-and-white print, see the [pen and ink drawing prompts](/blog/2026-05-01-master-ink-sketch-ai-art-prompts-pen-ink). For other styles, see the [AI art styles list](/blog/2025-11-30-ai-art-styles-guide).
