Lens terms are one of the fastest ways to change how an AI photo is framed. The model does not simulate glass and sensors. It has learned how photos described with "24mm" or "85mm" tend to look, so these words work as strong style and framing cues.

## Cheat sheet

| Prompt term | What it changes | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 14mm, ultra-wide lens | Very wide view, stretched edges, deep space | Interiors, architecture, dramatic landscapes |
| 24mm, wide-angle lens | Wide view with some depth exaggeration | Landscapes, street scenes, environmental portraits |
| 35mm lens | Natural, slightly wide view | Street, documentary, storytelling scenes |
| 50mm lens | Close to how we see | Everyday scenes, half-body portraits |
| 85mm lens | Tight framing, soft background | Head-and-shoulders portraits |
| 135mm / 200mm telephoto | Narrow view, background looks closer and larger | Portraits from a distance, sports |
| 400–600mm super telephoto | Very narrow, isolated subject | Wildlife, the moon behind a subject |
| 100mm macro lens | Extreme close-up of tiny details | Insects, droplets, textures |
| fisheye lens | Strong circular distortion | Skateboarding, playful or surreal shots |
| tilt-shift | Thin band of focus, miniature look | Cities and crowds that look like toy models |

Nikon's guide puts the rule simply: the longer the focal length, the narrower the angle of view and the higher the magnification.

## Copy-paste lens prompts

### Ultra-wide (14–16mm)

```text
a narrow cobblestone alley in Lisbon, 14mm ultra-wide lens, low angle, towering buildings leaning inward, morning light, deep perspective
```

### Wide-angle (24mm)

```text
a hiker standing on a ridge above a valley of clouds, 24mm wide-angle lens, vast landscape, sunrise, small figure for scale
```

### Street (35mm)

```text
a fruit seller arranging oranges at a busy market, 35mm lens, eye level, candid street photography, natural light, slight motion in the crowd
```

### Standard (50mm)

```text
a man reading a newspaper at a cafe table, 50mm lens, waist-up framing, soft window light, natural perspective
```

### Portrait (85mm)

```text
head-and-shoulders portrait of a young chef in a white jacket, 85mm lens, f/1.8, creamy background bokeh, warm kitchen lights blurred behind
```

### Telephoto (200mm)

```text
a runner crossing a city bridge at sunset, 200mm telephoto lens, compressed skyline looming behind, shallow depth of field
```

### Super telephoto (600mm)

```text
a red fox sitting in a snowy field, 600mm super telephoto lens, subject isolated against a smooth white background, wildlife photography
```

### Macro (100mm)

```text
a dewdrop on a blade of grass reflecting a sunrise, 100mm macro lens, extreme close-up, razor-thin focus, soft green background
```

### Fisheye

```text
a skateboarder mid-air over a concrete ramp, fisheye lens, curved horizon, blue sky, high-energy action shot
```

### Tilt-shift

```text
aerial view of a busy train station, tilt-shift photography, miniature effect, narrow band of focus, saturated colors
```

## Depth of field and aperture

Aperture terms control how blurred the background is. Use them together with the lens term.

```text
f/1.4, shallow depth of field, creamy bokeh, subject in sharp focus
```

```text
f/11, deep focus, everything sharp from foreground to background
```

A small f-number (f/1.4, f/1.8) means a blurred background. A large one (f/8, f/11, f/16) keeps the whole scene sharp, which suits landscapes and architecture.

## Camera angles and shot sizes

Angles often change an image more than the lens does:

```text
low angle shot, looking up at the subject, heroic and imposing
```

```text
high angle shot, looking down, subject appears small and vulnerable
```

```text
bird's-eye view, directly overhead, flat-lay composition
```

```text
dutch angle, tilted horizon, tense and uneasy mood
```

Shot sizes tell the model how much of the subject to show:

```text
extreme close-up, close-up, medium shot, full body shot, wide shot, establishing shot
```

## Tips

- **One lens per prompt.** "14mm, 85mm, telephoto" gives the model contradicting cues and the result drifts toward an average.
- **Pair the lens with a shot size.** "85mm, head-and-shoulders portrait" is more reliable than "85mm" alone.
- **The background follows the lens.** Wide lenses show more surroundings; telephoto lenses show less background and make it look closer and larger.
- **Camera brand names are only a style hint.** "Shot on a Canon EOS R5" may nudge the overall look, but it cannot reproduce that camera's actual output.
- **Remove conflicts in the negative prompt.** If you want everything sharp, add `bokeh, blurry background` to the negative field. See the [negative prompt lists](/blog/2025-11-29-stable-diffusion-negative-prompts-guide).
